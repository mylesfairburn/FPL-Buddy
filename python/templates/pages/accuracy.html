How accurate are the predictions?

Every prediction on this site is written down before the deadline and never recalculated. This page is what happened next: what the model said, what the players actually scored, and how far apart the two were.

How to read all this

The sample is picked, not random. These are squads somebody chose, and mostly squads the model chose for itself. Scoring well on players it rated highly is easier than scoring well on the whole pool, because most of the pool is players who never get near a pitch and are trivially projected at nearly nothing. So read these figures as how good are the projections for players worth considering — which is the useful question, but is not the same as a score over every player in the game.

The predictions are risk-adjusted. What gets stored against a pick is the chance the player features multiplied by what he would score if he did, so a doubtful player who sat out and scored nothing was partly anticipated. That is the fair comparison to make, and it is also why these numbers sit a little below the raw projections on a player's own page.

An average error of two points is not a small number when most players score between two and six. That is the honest state of predicting football over ninety minutes from a handful of inputs, and it is why the FAQ says the projections answer "who is the better pick" rather than "how many will he get". The rank correlation is the figure that speaks to the first question; the average error speaks to the second, and it is the weaker of the two.

Nothing on this page is recomputed when you load it. The predictions were frozen at their deadline and the actuals were written once the round settled, which is what makes the comparison worth publishing at all — see how the model works for why that freeze matters.
