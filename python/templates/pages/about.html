About {{ site_name }}

A free Fantasy Premier League tool that rates every player with a trained model, picks two AI squads a week, and shows you which teams have the kindest run of fixtures. Built by one person, for the same reason most FPL tools get built: wanting a better answer than a gut feeling.

How players are rated

Every player carries a projected points figure for each of the next eight gameweeks. That comes from a ridge regression trained separately for each position — goalkeepers, defenders, midfielders and forwards score points in genuinely different ways, so one model across all four would average away the thing you actually want to know.

The model is trained on per-gameweek data from 2025/26 onwards, and gains a season every year. Its inputs are a rolling three-gameweek average of expected goal involvements, minutes and bonus points, plus whether the fixture is home or away and how strong the opponent is. Rolling averages are shifted by one gameweek before use, so a player's own result can never leak into the features predicting it — the commonest way a model like this ends up looking far better than it is.

Two deliberate omissions. Total points is not an input, even though it is the most obviously predictive column available: it's Fantasy Premier League's own formula applied to minutes, goals and bonus, so including it alongside its own ingredients makes the model split credit between a total and the parts it was built from. And form is measured over three gameweeks rather than five or ten, because a longer window is steadier but slower to notice that something has changed.

The two AI squads

Both are picked by an integer linear program rather than by sorting a list. The difference matters because the constraints interact: "best points per million, position by position" can spend the budget early and then be unable to fill the last forward slot, or take three cheap defenders from one club and be blocked from the striker it actually wanted. A solver handles that directly and returns a squad that is provably the best available, not merely the best one a particular shortcut happened to find.

AI Best XI

The highest-scoring legal squad for the coming gameweek, rebuilt from scratch every week with no memory of the last one. £100m, two goalkeepers, five defenders, five midfielders, three forwards, and at most three players from any one club. It has no transfers to worry about, so it spends almost nothing on the bench — every spare pound belongs in the eleven.

AI Manager

A bot playing the actual game. It keeps one squad across the season, has a bank balance, earns and spends free transfers, takes four-point hits when a move justifies one, and holds a single set of chips.

It therefore judges players over the next several gameweeks rather than just the next one, weighted so that nearer fixtures count for more. That is the whole difference between the two: buy the best captain for one Saturday and sell him a fortnight later and you have burned four points and a free transfer for nothing. It also refuses to build a squad that can't field eleven players in a blank gameweek, and plans chips against the fixture list — double gameweeks and blanks are what actually make a chip worth playing, and both are visible months ahead.

Availability

Every player card carries a percentage. It's Fantasy Premier League's own published chance of playing, and the AI squads use it directly: a player rated 75% likely to feature is compared on 75% of his projection, not all of it. When the optimiser does start someone doubtful, it puts a dependable replacement on the bench in the same position, ordered so the automatic substitution actually covers them.

What this site cannot do

It cannot change your team. Fantasy Premier League's public API is read-only without an authenticated session, so everything here is a recommendation you then apply yourself in the official app. That is a deliberate limit, not a missing feature — this site never asks for your Fantasy Premier League password, and never will.

It also cannot tell the future. The projections are a model's best estimate from a handful of inputs, and football is not very predictable over ninety minutes. They're most useful for comparing players against each other, and least useful read as a forecast of anyone's actual score. The AI squads' predictions are frozen when each deadline passes and never recalculated, so the track record on each tab is an honest one rather than something quietly improved after the fact.

Not official

This is an unofficial fan project with no connection to the Premier League or to Fantasy Premier League. Player data, fixtures and results come from Fantasy Premier League's public API; team strength ratings come from ClubElo.

Questions, bugs and disagreements about the model are all welcome — see the contact page.
